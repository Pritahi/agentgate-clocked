Why this workflow?

Helpdesk assistants read tickets and knowledge, then suggest fixes. A malicious ticket can try to turn reading into tool use. Ordinary HR details can also leak into a broader handoff.

Research and what is different

Rezolve.ai's Gesa case study describes Teams support connected to ticketing and SharePoint. Snyk's Jira/Cursor analysis documents a poisoned support-ticket path. These establish workflow relevance, not a vulnerability in Rezolve.ai.

Detector wrappers already exist. AgentGate's small contribution is an explicit release contract: BLOCK or HOLD releases nothing; Protect's verified transformed copy is the only ticket export; scripts remain a separate human change review. No claim of new detection research.

OWASP recommends least privilege and human approval alongside detection.

Policy and threshold choices

Guard's API cutoff is 0.5. The app additionally holds benign verdicts at probability ≥0.35 for review; ≥0.65 or an injection verdict blocks. Inspect requires benign verdict and both malicious/suspicious probabilities <0.2 for eligibility. These are conservative prototype policies, not calibrated guarantees.

Missing verdicts, invalid spans, unchanged detected values, API errors and timeouts stop release. Original findings remain visible only in this review screen. Exports exclude them.
